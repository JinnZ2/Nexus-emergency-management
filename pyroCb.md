Pyro-cumulonimbus (pyroCb) downbursts are intense, localized downdrafts generated by fire‑fueled thunderstorms. Simulating them requires a coupled fire–atmosphere model that solves the non‑hydrostatic equations of motion, thermodynamics, and microphysics, with additional source terms for fire heat, moisture, and aerosols. Below are the key variables, governing equations, and diagnostic/downburst‑specific relationships used in high‑resolution numerical simulations.

2. Core Governing Equations

2.1 Mass Continuity

Compressible form:

\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0


Anelastic approximation (common in cloud models):

\nabla \cdot (\rho_0 \mathbf{u}) = 0


where \rho_0(z) is a height‑dependent reference density.

2.2 Momentum Equations

The three components, including buoyancy and turbulence, are:

\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p' + \mathbf{B} - 2\mathbf{\Omega}\times\mathbf{u} + \frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\cdot \tau

In the vertical, the buoyancy term is:

B = -g\frac{\rho'}{\rho_0} \approx g\left( \frac{\theta'}{\theta_0} + 0.61 q_v' - q_c - q_r - q_i - q_s - q_g \right)

Here g is gravitational acceleration, \rho' is density perturbation, and primes denote deviations from a hydrostatic base state. The first two terms in parentheses represent thermal and moisture buoyancy; the last terms represent water loading (precipitation drag).

For large‑eddy simulation (LES), the turbulent stress \tau is typically parameterised using a subgrid‑scale model (e.g., Smagorinsky or 1.5‑order TKE closure).

2.3 Thermodynamic Equation (Potential Temperature)

\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta = \left(\frac{\theta}{T}\right)\frac{L_v}{c_p}\left(\frac{\delta q_v}{dt}\right)_{\text{phase}} + \frac{\theta}{c_p T}Q_{\text{rad}} + \frac{\theta}{c_p T}Q_{\text{fire}} + \nabla\cdot(K_h\nabla\theta)

· L_v = latent heat of vaporisation
· c_p = specific heat at constant pressure
· Q_{\text{fire}} = sensible heat flux from the fire (W m⁻³ when distributed over a model layer)
· K_h = eddy diffusivity for heat.

2.4 Moisture Conservation Equations

For each water species q_x:

\frac{\partial q_x}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla q_x = S_x + \nabla\cdot(K_h\nabla q_x)

S_x represents microphysical sources/sinks (condensation, evaporation, melting, collection, etc.). For water vapour q_v, the source includes evaporation of rain and cloud water; for rain, it includes autoconversion, accretion, evaporation, etc.

A simple bulk warm‑rain parameterisation (Kessler type) gives:

· Autoconversion (cloud → rain):
  S_{r,\text{auto}} = \alpha (q_c - q_{c,\text{crit}}) \quad \text{if } q_c > q_{c,\text{crit}}
· Accretion (cloud by rain):
  S_{c,\text{accr}} = -\gamma q_c q_r
· Evaporation of rain:
  S_{v,\text{evap}} = \beta (q_{vs} - q_v) q_r^{0.85}


  where q_{vs} is the saturation mixing ratio.

2.5 Turbulence Kinetic Energy (TKE) Equation

\frac{\partial e}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla e = \mathcal{P}_{\text{shear}} + \mathcal{P}_{\text{buoy}} - \epsilon + \nabla\cdot(K_e\nabla e)

· Shear production: \mathcal{P}_{\text{shear}} = K_m |\nabla_h \mathbf{u}|^2
· Buoyancy production: \mathcal{P}_{\text{buoy}} = -\frac{g}{\theta_0}K_h\frac{\partial \theta_v}{\partial z}
· Dissipation: \epsilon = C_\epsilon \frac{e^{3/2}}{l}
· l is a turbulent length scale, K_m and K_e are eddy viscosities.

3. Fire‑Coupled Source Terms

Fire spread is often parameterised with the Rothermel surface‑fire model or a physics‑based approach. The key variables are:

· Fuel load, fuel moisture, wind speed, slope.
· Heat release per unit area: H_f (W m⁻²).

The atmospheric heat source is:

Q_{\text{fire}} = \frac{H_f}{\rho c_p \Delta z}

applied over the lowest model layers, where \Delta z is the layer thickness.

Fire‑generated moisture (from combustion) and aerosol (smoke) can be added as sources to q_v and an aerosol concentration variable.

---

4. Downburst‑Specific Equations

A downburst is driven by negative buoyancy from evaporative cooling and precipitation loading. The vertical momentum equation for the downdraft can be simplified to:

\frac{dw}{dt} = -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial p'}{\partial z} - g\left(\frac{\theta_\rho'}{\theta_{\rho0}} + q_r + q_c + q_i\right) - C_d |w|w

· \theta_\rho' is the virtual potential temperature perturbation (cooling due to evaporation makes it negative).
· The second term is total negative buoyancy (thermal + water loading).
· The last term is a simple drag parameterisation due to precipitation particles.

In the quasi‑steady limit (neglecting acceleration), the maximum downdraft speed can be estimated from the vertical integral of negative buoyancy:

w_{d,\max} \approx \sqrt{2 \int_{z_s}^{z_0} B_{\text{neg}}\, dz}

where

B_{\text{neg}} = g\left( \frac{\theta_{\text{env}} - \theta_{\text{parcel}}}{\theta_{\text{env}}} + q_r + q_c \right)

Here \theta_{\text{parcel}} includes evaporative cooling as rain falls into a dry sub‑cloud layer, and q_r, q_c represent water loading. This gives a first‑order estimate of the downdraft intensity.

When the downdraft reaches the ground, it spreads horizontally. The maximum surface wind gust can be approximated using a simplified impinging jet model (e.g., Proctor 1988):

V_{\text{gust}} \approx \alpha\, w_{d,\max}

where \alpha is an empirical factor often between 0.5 and 1.0, depending on downdraft diameter, height of origin, and ambient wind.

---

5. Numerical Simulation Approach

For realistic simulations, state‑of‑the‑art coupled models such as WRF‑Fire, CAWFE, or LES with fire spread are used. They solve the full compressible non‑hydrostatic equations with:

· Grid spacing of 50–500 m (or finer) to resolve the pyroCb and downburst.
· Bulk microphysics schemes (e.g., Morrison, Thompson).
· PBL/turbulence schemes (e.g., MYNN, LES SGS).
· Fire‑atmosphere coupling: heat and moisture fluxes from the fire inject buoyancy and water vapour, which can trigger deep convection.

---

6. Summary of Essential Equations for a Simplified Downburst Simulation

If one wants a simplified analytical or 1‑D column model of a pyroCb downburst, the essential equations are:

1. Vertical momentum
   \frac{\partial w}{\partial t} = -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial p'}{\partial z} + B - C_d|w|w
2. Potential temperature tendency (including evaporative cooling)
   \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial t} = -w\frac{\partial \theta}{\partial z} - \frac{L_v}{c_p}\frac{\theta}{T}\left(\frac{\partial q_v}{\partial t}\right)_{\text{evap}} + Q_{\text{fire}}
3. Rain water mixing ratio tendency (with evaporation)
   \frac{\partial q_r}{\partial t} = -w\frac{\partial q_r}{\partial z} - \left(\frac{\partial q_v}{\partial t}\right)_{\text{evap}} - V_t\frac{\partial q_r}{\partial z}


   where V_t is the terminal fall speed of rain.
4. Saturation adjustment to compute condensation/evaporation.

These can be integrated vertically to estimate downdraft speed and surface gust potential.
